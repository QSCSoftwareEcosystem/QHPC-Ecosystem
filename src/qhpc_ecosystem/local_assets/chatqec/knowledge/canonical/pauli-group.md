# Pauli Group

The Pauli group is the algebraic backbone of stabilizer quantum error
correction. On a single qubit it is generated by the Pauli matrices $X$, $Y$,
$Z$ together with phase factors; on $n$ qubits it is the group of all tensor
products of single-qubit Paulis with an overall phase. Stabilizer codes,
syndrome measurement, and logical operators are all defined as subsets of this
group, which is why almost every QEC construction starts here.

## Single-qubit Paulis

The single-qubit Pauli matrices are

$$
X = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Y = \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Z = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},
$$

with $I$ the identity. They are Hermitian, unitary, square to $I$, and any two
distinct non-identity Paulis anticommute (e.g. $XZ = -ZX$). The single-qubit
Pauli group is $\mathcal{P}_1 = \{\pm 1, \pm i\}\times\{I, X, Y, Z\}$; the phase
factors are needed to make it closed under multiplication ($XY = iZ$).

## The n-qubit Pauli group

The $n$-qubit Pauli group $\mathcal{P}_n$ consists of all operators

$$
P = i^{\lambda}\, P_1 \otimes P_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes P_n,
\qquad P_j \in \{I, X, Y, Z\},\ \lambda \in \{0,1,2,3\}.
$$

Ignoring phase, there are $4^n$ distinct Pauli strings. Two key facts drive all
of stabilizer QEC:

- **Any two elements either commute or anticommute.** $P$ and $Q$ commute iff
  they overlap on an *even* number of qubits with anticommuting single-qubit
  factors.
- **Weight.** The *weight* of a Pauli string is the number of non-identity
  tensor factors; it measures how many qubits an error touches and sets the
  scale for code distance (see [[logical-operators]]).

## Symplectic (binary) representation

Discarding phase, each Pauli string maps to a length-$2n$ binary vector
$(\mathbf{x}\,|\,\mathbf{z})$, where $x_j = 1$ if $P_j \in \{X, Y\}$ and
$z_j = 1$ if $P_j \in \{Y, Z\}$. Multiplication becomes addition mod 2, and the
commutation relation becomes the **symplectic inner product**

$$
\langle P, Q\rangle = \mathbf{x}_P\!\cdot\!\mathbf{z}_Q + \mathbf{z}_P\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}_Q \pmod 2,
$$

which is $0$ when $P, Q$ commute and $1$ when they anticommute. This linear-algebra
picture over $\mathbb{F}_2$ is what makes stabilizer codes efficiently
describable and simulable (Gottesman–Knill).

## Why it matters for QEC

- Stabilizers are an abelian subgroup of $\mathcal{P}_n$ not containing $-I$
  (see [[stabilizer-formalism]]).
- Errors of interest are Pauli strings, so error correction reduces to tracking
  cosets of the stabilizer group; general noise is handled by twirling to a
  Pauli channel (see [[pauli-channels]]).
- Logical operators are Paulis that commute with every stabilizer but lie
  outside the group (see [[logical-operators]]).
